# Decisions

Each entry records what was chosen and — more importantly — **what was rejected and why**.
If you are about to suggest something in a "Rejected" list, read the reason first. These were
argued through already.

---

### D-01 · ChordPro is the canonical format

Chords inline in text. Transposition becomes a pure string operation, and one source renders
as chords, lyrics-only, or Nashville numbers.

**Rejected:** PDF/image as source of truth (cannot be transposed at all — this is the
decision the whole app rests on). A custom JSON music format (no benefit, high exit cost;
ChordPro is a de facto standard readable by OnSong, SongBook, Chordii, so the library
survives this app's death). MusicXML/full notation (solves a problem we don't have).

---

### D-02 · Key lives on `service_item`, not `song`

The same song is played in different keys depending on who leads. Same for `capo`.

**Rejected:** key as a property of the song. Guarantees a schema rewrite within months.

---

### D-03 · Three-level model: Song → Arrangement → ServiceItem

`song` is the work (title, author, CCLI#). `arrangement` is a specific chart — the church's
version with an extra bridge differs from the published one. `service_item` is that
arrangement in a specific service, in a specific key.

---

### D-04 · No OMR. Chord symbols and lyrics only.

Optical Music Recognition (reading staves and noteheads) is an unsolved research problem and
performs badly on photocopies. The feature set needs chord symbols, lyrics, and section
structure. **Notation is discardable** — extract the chord symbols above the staff and the
lyrics below it, throw the rest away, and the app works perfectly.

**Rejected:** Audiveris/MusicXML pipeline. Enormous effort for data we don't use.

---

### D-05 · Scans are retained forever and shown in-app

Original scan stays attached to the arrangement permanently, one tap from the chart view.

This changes the trust model: ChordPro is a *derived transposition layer over an
authoritative source document*. An extraction error isn't corruption, it's a typo sitting
next to the correct answer.

Handwritten annotations ("capo 3", "no bridge tonight") are often more valuable than the
printed content — they encode the church's actual arrangement. Don't parse them; preserve
the image. Scan **grayscale, not bitonal**, or faint pencil is destroyed.

Store PDF as canonical archive, per-page WebP derivatives for the viewer.

---

### D-06 · No batch review queue. Correction is inline.

Every rendered chord is tappable — tap, fix, done in three seconds. Errors surface at
Wednesday practice, where the person who notices is the person who can fix it, and they know
how the song actually goes.

**Rejected:** a dedicated review UI and a 2.5-hour solo verification pass. Deleted an entire
screen from the build.

**Rejected:** two-pass consensus extraction (running two different extractors and diffing
them to catch silent errors). Priced against errors surfacing *live on Sunday*. With practice
night as the safety net and the scan one tap away, paying double to catch an error that costs
three seconds to fix isn't worth it. Single pass, plus the cheap local validators.

Requires: loose permissions (12 people who know each other — anyone can edit) and an
append-only revision log so a bad edit is one undo away.

---

### D-07 · Verification states: `unverified` / `verified` / `flagged`

Import defaults to `unverified`. `verified` requires one deliberate human tap. `flagged`
means someone found a problem but couldn't fix it in the moment (they're holding a bass) —
that's the real work queue, generated by people who found real problems.

**Played ≠ verified.** A song can be played entirely off projected lyrics with nobody
checking the scan. Track `times_played`/`last_played_at` automatically off the setlist join;
track `verified_at`/`verified_by` only on explicit action.

Surface the badge **in the setlist builder**, not just the chart view — Wednesday is when it
can still change what you do; Sunday on the platform is too late.

---

### D-08 · Digitization is a local batch script, not an app subsystem

It runs once against the filing cabinet. Run it on the laptop the scanner is attached to;
`rsync` output to the rack. No queue, no worker, no container — a folder of files and a
script.

**Rejected:** OCR on serverless. CPU-bound and slow; would burn the monthly compute budget
and hit execution timeouts. **Rejected:** OCR on EC2/Batch — provisioning a cluster for a job
that runs once on hardware already owned.

Calling a hosted OCR/VLM *API* from the local script is fine — that's a vendor call, not
infrastructure.

Iteration is why local wins: extraction will be re-run many times as prompts and regexes get
fixed. Locally that's `python digitize.py`. Any cloud arrangement means re-uploading 600 MB
each iteration.

---

### D-09 · Manual page splitting

Sheet-fed scanning produces one large PDF. Build a dumb splitter: page thumbnails in a grid,
click the first page of each song. ~30 minutes of clicking for 300 songs, 100% accurate.

**Rejected:** automatic title-detection boundary heuristics. An afternoon of tuning and you
still have to check the output.

---

### D-10 · Free tier is sufficient, indefinitely

Cloud stores text only: ~2 MB of ChordPro, ~2 MB of embeddings, trivial relational data.
12 users will not dent any transfer or compute limit. Scans live on the church's existing
server rack.

Notes: Vercel Hobby is non-commercial-only, but Vercel staff have confirmed nonprofit work by
unpaid volunteers qualifies — **this flips to commercial the moment anyone is paid.**
Supabase free pauses projects after 7 days of inactivity, which a church's calendar can
trigger; use a cron ping, or Neon (auto-wakes, no manual restore).

Copyright note: where bytes sit at rest is not the variable that matters — access scope and
CCLI licensing are. Don't reason about hosting geography as a copyright control.

---

### D-11 · Framework choice is coupled to hosting — decided: Vercel + Next.js

- **On Vercel:** Next is fine. Not overengineered. User already knows it.
- **Self-hosted on the rack:** switch to a static Vite SPA. Next self-hosted means running a
  Node process 24/7 for an app entirely behind a login; static files served by Caddy have
  far less to go wrong.

**Rejected:** a rack + AWS hybrid. Two operational surfaces, two credential sets, an AWS
account with surprise-bill risk, for no capability either pure option lacks.

Decided 2026-08-25: hosting on Vercel, so Next.js stays. Revisit if hosting ever moves to the
rack.

---

### D-12 · Postgres + pgvector; no vector database

300 songs is under half a megabyte of embeddings. pgvector is available on every Neon plan
(`CREATE EXTENSION vector`). **Do not build an HNSW or IVFFlat index** — brute-force exact
search over 300 rows returns in single-digit milliseconds with perfect recall, and skipping
the index also sidesteps pgvector's ~2000-dimension index limit.

**Rejected:** Pinecone or any dedicated vector service. New failure mode, zero benefit.

---

### D-13 · Embed enriched descriptions, not raw lyrics

Worship lyrics share an enormous common vocabulary ("Lord", "praise", "holy", "grace").
Embedding them directly clusters everything into one blob and ranks near-randomly.

Instead: a one-time enrichment pass produces structured themes, scripture references,
emotional posture, and liturgical function per song. Embed *that*. It also gives ordinary
search ("communion songs", "something about lament") which is probably used more than the
sermon workflow.

Strip ChordPro markup before embedding.

---

### D-14 · Theme matching: theme similarity is a filter, not a ranking

A thematically perfect song can be functionally wrong — a 6/8 ballad can't open a service.
Rank by: liturgical function/energy fit for the slot, recency penalty (downrank anything
played in the last 6–8 weeks), whether the team knows it (`times_played > 0`), and key
adjacency to neighboring songs.

The real value is **rediscovery** of the ~240 archived songs nobody remembers, not the ~60 in
active rotation. This is why the archive is worth digitizing.

Sermon notes may arrive as WIP, and the topic firms up before the prose does. Regeneration
must be cheap and idempotent — run whenever new info arrives, latest wins. Never a one-shot
trigger that locks.

---

### D-15 · Scheduling is deprioritized

Coordinating 7 musicians is a group text. Nobody at this size is drowning in roster
complexity. Ship library + transposition + shareable setlist; see whether anyone asks for
scheduling. They may not.

Keep `key_override` on `service_item` regardless — that's what makes setlists work at all.

---

### D-16 · OCR: Tesseract + geometry, not VLM

DOMAIN.md §7 already specifies the algorithm chord-line/lyric-line alignment actually needs:
take each chord token's x-center, find which character in the lyric line beneath it occupies
that x-range, splice `[Chord]` in at that index. That's the whole trick — flattened OCR text
destroys the horizontal alignment that says a chord lands on "grace" and not "sweet." A VLM
asked to transcribe the page is implicitly flattening unless very carefully prompted to
preserve position, which reintroduces the exact failure mode the geometric approach was
designed to avoid.

Also consistent with D-08: no per-page vendor API cost, fully deterministic and testable, and
no CCLI-licensed sheet content leaves the building.

**Accepted tradeoff:** Tesseract's raw accuracy on handwriting, pencil annotations, and faded
old photocopies is weaker than a VLM's. This is exactly what the Phase 1.5 pilot (20 charts,
including the worst photocopies) is for — catch it on the pilot batch, not after committing to
scanning all ~300.
